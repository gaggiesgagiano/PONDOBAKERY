Money & accounts keeps the books: where the money is (safe, bank, EcoCash), which suppliers we still owe, the VAT and what we owe ZIMRA and NSSA, and the balance sheet. The tabs are Overview · Cash book · Suppliers we owe · VAT · Balance sheet · Accounts set-up. Only people with the Money & accounts right see it – it shows the owner's money.

Type once. Most money comes in by itself from where it was already typed: cash-ups (cash to the safe, swipe and bank transfers to the bank, EcoCash to EcoCash), expenses paid from the bank, supplier payments, final pay runs, staff advances and customer invoices marked paid. Under + Money in / out you type only what Pondo can't know.

1 · Set up the accounts once – the Manager, on the day the books start

  1. Accounts set-up → + Add an account for every place money is kept: Safe (cash in the safe), the bank account and EcoCash.
  2. Give each the start date (the first day of the books – best opening day or the 1st of a month) and the balance on that day: what is really in the safe, the bank balance on the statement, the EcoCash wallet balance.
  3. Equipment in the register and the last final stock take on that day count as the owner's / boss's investment at the start – they are not profit.

2 · Money in / out whenever it happens

  • Owner / boss put money in – capital he does not want back. If it must be paid back: Loan to the business, and later Loan paid back.
  • Move money between accounts – e.g. the manager takes safe cash to the bank: from Safe to Bank. Use the deposit slip number as the ref.
  • Paid ZIMRA – PAYE, Paid ZIMRA – VAT, Paid NSSA, Paid ZIMDEF – so the amounts owed go down.
  • Bank / EcoCash charges and Interest – from the statement.
  • Bought equipment – also add the item to the equipment register. Equipment the boss pays for himself: type Owner / boss put money in and Bought equipment on the same account and day.
  • Owner took money out (drawings).
  • Not here: sales, cash-ups, expenses, stock, wages, advances – they are already in. Typing them again counts them twice.

3 · Suppliers we owe every GRV · pay on time

  1. Give every supplier its payment terms (Stock → Suppliers): 0 = we pay on delivery, 30 = within 30 days. The due date of every GRV comes from it.
  2. Every GRV is an invoice we owe until it is paid. Suppliers we owe shows each supplier with not due yet · 1–30 · 31–60 · over 60 days late, and every unpaid invoice.
  3. To pay: Pay a supplier → supplier → date, paid from (bank, safe, EcoCash, or owner paid from own pocket), amount and the proof-of-payment number → Fill oldest first (or type per invoice) → Save payment. The invoices must add up to the amount paid.
  4. Each supplier has a statement (click the name): invoices, payments and what we owe. Compare it with the supplier's own statement every month.
  5. The GRV page shows Paid · Part paid · Not paid yet. A wrong payment can only be deleted by the Manager.

4 · Check the bank and count the safe every month end – at least

  1. Open the account → Check statement (bank, EcoCash) or Safe count (the safe – a manager and one other person count).
  2. Type the date and the closing balance on the statement (or the cash counted) → Compare.
  3. When it differs, look for what is missing: bank charges, a deposit not typed as a move, a supplier paid but not typed (or typed twice), a wrong cash-up, money the boss put in. Type it, compare again, and write what you found in the notes.

5 · VAT every VAT period

The VAT worksheet shows VAT on sales (till, days typed by hand, tax invoices) minus VAT on GRVs and expenses = what we owe ZIMRA, and what is still unpaid. It is a worksheet: your accountant confirms the rates and files the return. Input VAT can only be claimed with a proper tax invoice – keep every supplier invoice with its GRV. When VAT is paid, type Paid ZIMRA – VAT.

6 · Balance sheet month end · for the accountant

What the business owns (money in the accounts, stock from the last final stock take, unpaid customer invoices, staff advances, equipment at book value) minus what it owes (suppliers, VAT, PAYE, NSSA, ZIMDEF, loans, money the owner paid from his own pocket) = what the business is worth. It is made up of the investment at the start, capital put in since, less drawings, and the profit kept – compare that with the month-end profit. A big difference means something was not typed.

7 · Month end – the order first days of the new month

  1. All daily sales, cash-ups (signed off), expenses and GRVs typed.
  2. The Saturday / month-end stock take finalised.
  3. Pay run finalised; PAYE, NSSA and ZIMDEF payments typed when paid.
  4. Bank statement checked and safe counted – both agree.
  5. Supplier statements compared; payments typed.
  6. Reports → Accountant's month pack → Excel: income statement, VAT, purchases, payroll, invoices, equipment, cash book, suppliers owed and the balance sheet – send it to the accountant. Then Lock this month.
